Update (2026 Aug 21, +2 G37 obs, arc 9.8 hr): the refit (perigee 535,983 ± 527 km, e=0.776±0.002, P=426.5 d, U=9.9) tightens the clone velocity uncertainty 13-fold and revises the forecast: capture was 2026 Apr 28 (v∞ 0.075 km/s), this is a single-loop capture — the earlier Dec 7 second-perigee prediction is retracted; the object reaches ~1.9M km apogee in October, returns only to ~1.2M km in mid-December, and escapes 2026 Dec 29 ± 2 d. Observable now through ~Aug 26 (V<21), then V≈22.4–22.9 through the fall. The deep-backward integration now pins its previous Earth departure to spring 2015 (median 2015 Apr 19) after one full co-orbital lap — the only known low-energy deep-space departure in that window is the DSCOVR launch (2015-007, 2015 Feb 11), whose Falcon 9 second stage has been in an untracked heliocentric orbit since 2015. The stage itself is photometrically excluded (H≈26.4–28 vs 31.5), so the working hypothesis is meter-class debris from the DSCOVR launch, or an uncatalogued object departing in the same window.
