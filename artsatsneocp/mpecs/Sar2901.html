Identification analysis (2026 Aug 20)

MPC removed Sar2901 from the NEOCP as artificial (no designation; purged from Scout). The object is a temporarily captured weak-stability-boundary loiterer: 24 clones sampled from the find_orb covariance agree it was captured (geocentric two-body energy E<0) around 2026 May 24 at ~2.2M km with capture v∞ ≈ 0.07 km/s, made the discovery perigee on Aug 17–18 at ~527,000 km, and will make a second, deeper perigee near 2026 Dec 7 at ~233,000 km (observable Nov 28 – Dec 14, peaking near V ≈ 18.5) before escaping around 2027 Jan — an eight-month, two-loop episode closely resembling 2020 SO's 2020–21 capture (in at 0.26 km/s, two perigees, out at 0.34 km/s).

All catalogued candidates were tested and excluded: every full-size rocket stage (DART / DSCOVR / IMAP Falcon 9 S2, CZ-3B lunar stages, New Glenn GS2) is H ≈ 25–28, i.e. 25–60× too bright for Sar2901's H = 31.5; all fresh Space-Track TLEs and all 59 JPL Horizons spacecraft with valid ephemerides miss by 26–147°. The closest physical match was the lost CAPSTONE kick stage Lunar Photon (2022-070C = NORAD 52916; Bill Gray's fit: H = 31.59, WSB orbit q=256k km, Q=1.56M km, lost 2023 Mar) — but the joint propagation below rules it out: the Earth-distance curves of Photon-forward (from 2023) and Sar2901-backward (from 2026) cross on 2024-12-30 at 72.8M km with only a 0.2M km radial gap, yet the 3D check shows the two tracks are then 139° apart in orbital phase (0.91 AU separation) — same distance, opposite sides of Earth. Sar2901 and Lunar Photon are different objects; the identity of Sar2901 remains open.

Observable now through ~Aug 26 (V<21), then V≈22.4–22.9 through the fall. The deep-backward integration now pins its previous Earth departure to spring 2015 (median 2015 Apr 19) after one full co-orbital lap — the only known low-energy deep-space departure in that window is the DSCOVR launch (2015-007, 2015 Feb 11), whose Falcon 9 second stage has been in an untracked heliocentric orbit since 2015. The stage itself is photometrically excluded (H≈26.4–28 vs 31.5), so the working hypothesis is meter-class debris from the DSCOVR launch, or an uncatalogued object departing in the same window.
