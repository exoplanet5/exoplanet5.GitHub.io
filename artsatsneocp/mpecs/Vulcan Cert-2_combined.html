Linkage, non-gravitational fit and returns (2026 Oct 25)

C43KHJ1 and CDV1NN2 are the Centaur V upper stage of ULA's Vulcan Cert-2 flight (2024-179A = NORAD 61448), launched 2024 Oct 4 11:25 UTC from Cape Canaveral. C43KHJ1: 32 observations 2024 Oct 5.25–5.99 (18–36 h after liftoff), at 0.0025–0.005 AU. On its own it fits a geocentric escape hyperbola: e = 1.062, perigee 7,092 km on Oct 4 12:00:50 TT (about 35 min after liftoff, at the escape burn), i = 26.6°. CDV1NN2: 18 observations 2025 Oct 26–Nov 16 at about 0.04 AU, V ≈ 20.5–21.5. Fitted on its own, its heliocentric orbit is a = 0.99202 AU, e = 0.0475, i = 0.034°, P = 360.9 d. That is the orbit Bill Gray fitted to the 2024 departure (a = 0.99199, e = 0.0513, i = 0.036°, P = 360.9 d).

Why non-gravitational terms are needed. A gravity-only joint fit (DE440, all planets and the Moon, solar GR) reaches 34/50 obs at 0.47″, but only by rejecting the 2025 Oct 26 G96 tracklet, which sits 19″ off. A radial solar-radiation-pressure term does not help. Every SRP fit wants a negative AMR (about −0.02 m²/kg, unphysical) and still leaves Oct 26 12″ off. The orbit lies in the ecliptic (i = 0.03°), so Sun-radial pressure cannot push it out of plane, yet the link needs an out-of-plane push. The fit above uses find_orb's 3-parameter model (A1 radial, A2 transverse, A3 normal, 1/r²): A3 = (−3.4 ± 0.75)×10−9 AU/d² (≈ −7×10−8 m/s²), 32/50 obs at 0.32″. find_orb still drops the Oct 26 tracklet (6″), the early 703/T05/M22 obs and M54.

An independent DE440 N-body fitter, which reproduces find_orb's gravity-only residuals to 0.01″, fits both apparitions with a single impulse of about 0.4 m/s. Placed on 2025 Jan 25 (R −0.35, T −0.03, N −0.15 m/s), it fits all observations except 703/T05/M54 at 0.35″, Oct 26 included. The data cannot date the impulse: any date from 2024 Nov to 2025 Aug gives 0.35–0.8″ with |Δv| = 0.2–0.7 m/s, while an impulse in the first week after launch fails. Positions alone cannot separate one impulse from gradual venting or boil-off of residual propellant (Centaur V is a LH2/LOX stage) or from attitude-dependent radiation pressure on the long cylindrical stage. The 703/T05 obs from 18–24 h after launch sit 2–7″ from every smooth model, which is consistent with the stage still venting on Oct 5.

Cross-check with Bill Gray's hex TLEs (24179a.tle: 75 obs 2024 Oct 5–11; 24179a25.tle: 21 obs 2025 Oct 19–Nov 16), evaluated with get_vect at each TLE epoch: the 2025-only fit matches 24179a25 in direction to 0.0–0.3″ over the observed interval (range differs by about 1,800 km). The 2024-only fit is within 8–12″ of 24179a through 2024 Dec (a 1-day arc against Bill's 6-day arc). Propagated forward with gravity only, Bill's 2024 orbit lands 13′ from the 2025 positions. The best joint fits agree with 24179a25 in direction to ≤ 1″ but place the 2025 stage about 18,000 km farther along the line of sight, a range shift that the one-year link imposes. Bill's 2025 TLE propagated forward gives the same 2026 and 2029 encounters as the table below.

Close Earth flyby, 2029 Feb 27. The stage's period (361–363 d) is within a few days of Earth's year, so it stays near Earth, and the 2026 and 2028 passes lead into a slow (v∞ = 1.83 km/s) encounter on 2029 Feb 27 at about 07–10 h TT. Every joint fit has the stage passing Earth at a geocentric 8,100–17,800 km (altitude 1,700–11,400 km). At such a slow approach Earth's gravity bends the path strongly, and the capture radius in the B-plane is 39,300 km. The cross-track coordinate ξ ≈ 38,800 km is almost the same in every model, so an Earth entry would need the timing coordinate |ζ| ≲ 3,500 km. The joint fits give ζ = +21,700 to +24,400 km (impulse models) and +42,000 to +57,000 km (constant acceleration). None of 500 covariance clones enters. A 2025-only orbit has ζ = −20,000 km (16–84%: −42,600 to −1,700), and 30 of 200 such clones fall inside the capture radius. That is why Bill's 2025 elements list perigee q = 8,866 ± 4,574 km. The 2024 link moves ζ to the other side. Solar pressure after 2025 (AMR 0.005–0.02) moves ζ a further +2,000 to +9,000 km for the joint solution. Unmodeled venting from 2025 to 2029 is the main remaining unknown, and astrometry in 2026 Dec will settle it. Near perigee the stage would be about V 11–15 at phase angles 126–149°, outside Earth's shadow. Its heliocentric orbit after the flyby depends on the model (P 353–429 d, i 1.5–2.1°), so later returns cannot be predicted yet.
