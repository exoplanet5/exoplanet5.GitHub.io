Signal-to-noise ratio

All terms are electrons in the stacked image of N frames of t seconds (T = Nt):

SNR=S⋆S⋆+npix(bskyT+IdarkT+NσRN2) S⋆=10−0.4mF0AeffToptQE10−0.4kXfapT,bsky=10−0.4μF0AeffToptQEs2

F0 is the band's photon zero point (photons s⁻¹ m⁻² for a magnitude-0 source through the peak-normalised band response), Aeff = πD²(1 − ε²)/4, s the pixel scale (″/px) and μ the sky brightness at the target. The sky is measured from the ground, so it is not attenuated by the extinction term; only the source is. With a measured zero point ZP the instrumental terms are replaced by 100.4(ZP − m)·10−0.4k(X−1) for the source and 100.4(ZP + k − μ) per arcsec² for the sky.

PSF, pixels and trailing

For PSF-fit photometry fap = 1 and npix is the noise-equivalent area npix = 1 / ΣPij² of the pixel-integrated, trailed PSF, averaged over sub-pixel position. For a well-sampled Gaussian this is 2.266 FWHM²/s²; a Moffat profile with β = 4.765 (Kolmogorov seeing) has 3.02 FWHM², i.e. real seeing costs ≈ 0.15 mag against a Gaussian of the same FWHM in the sky-limited regime. For aperture photometry npix is the aperture area (a stadium of radius r around the trail) and fap the PSF flux inside it. A target moving at ω ″/s relative to the tracking is smeared over L = ωt in each frame; the trailed PSF is evaluated exactly for a Gaussian and through an equivalent Gaussian for a Moffat (checked against a brute-force integration, within 2.5 %). In the long-trail limit the trailing loss reproduces Veres & Chesley (2017).

The three solve modes

Limiting magnitude. With background variance B fixed, SNR² (S + B) = S² gives S = [SNR² + √(SNR⁴ + 4 SNR² B)]/2, then m = −2.5 log10(S/S0). SNR. Evaluated directly. Exposure time. For a point source S = αT, B = βT + δ (δ = Nσ²RNnpix), a quadratic in T; for a moving target npix grows with t, so the equation is solved numerically, and the SNR of a long-trailed target saturates — the calculator reports when the requested SNR cannot be reached at any exposure.

Atmosphere and sky

Air mass from Pickering (2002), finite to the horizon. Dark-sky zenith values per band come from the Rubin Observatory Cerro Pachón sky spectrum and extinction from its MODTRAN X = 1 atmosphere (lsst-pst/syseng_throughputs), integrated through each filter curve; these reproduce Rubin's published sky, extinction and zero points. Away from the zenith the sky brightens by X·10−0.4k(X−1) (Krisciunas & Schaefer 1991). Moonlight adds the classic Kitt Peak UBVRI brightening by lunar age, interpolated in wavelength — an average; the real value depends on Moon–target distance and lunar altitude.

Validation

Rubin SMTN-002 (2026-08-12) single-visit 5σ depths are reproduced to within 0.02 mag in all six LSST bands (example situation “Rubin LSST · 30-s visit”). Band zero points match Rubin's 1-s zero points to 0.004 mag and the Gaia DR3 zero points to 0.017 mag. The test suite (tests/) also checks the PSF geometry against a 16× supersampled brute-force integration, the three-mode round trips on 400 random systems, and the read-, sky- and source-limited scaling laws.
