A letter describes where an object is now, not where it was sent. A status cell carries one letter for each object it describes. A spacecraft still working at its operational orbit gets no letter unless that orbit is itself a long-lived Earth–Moon residence (C), or it is derelict. R and I are terminal. W takes precedence over H; once a forecast return has passed without capture the object reverts to H, or stays W for its next return — DART's F9 stage after its 2026-05-11 pass at 2.7 million km is the example.

Returns come in three kinds: Earth co-orbital stages that drift back on synodic periods of years to decades (most of the W list); weakly bound Earth–Sun–Moon manifold objects such as J002E3 and 2020 SO, which can be temporarily recaptured; and stages put on fast-clearing disposal paths that turned out Earth-resonant — MMX's H3 stage completes 7 orbits in almost exactly 10 years and returns in October 2036.

1.2 Sources and authority

  1. Space-Track SATCAT DECAY + COMMENT — final word on the end of an object's geocentric track. A DECAY date with an empty comment is an atmospheric reentry. Space-Track also sets DECAY when a payload lands on or hits the Moon (LUNAR LANDING, LUNAR IMPACT) or departs to a libration point (L1 LAGRANGIAN ORBIT — IMAP, its two companions and its Falcon 9 stage all carry DECAY 2025-09-26), so the comment, not the date alone, decides the bucket. Comments without a DECAY date (HELIOCENTRIC ORBIT (SUN), L2 LAGRANGIAN ORBIT, SELENOCENTRIC ORBIT (MOON)) are labels set near launch and are often stale.
  2. JPL Horizons SPK ephemerides — mission navigation data, authoritative up to the last tracking date given in the target header; later dates are predictions.
  3. Bill Gray's TLE archive ~/tles/ — 672 hex/classical TLE files behind 838 # Include directives in tle_list.txt, plus his Warn lines. A year suffix (13070b25.tle) gives the year of the fit.
  4. Space-Track GP element sets — used only when the epoch is less than one year old; an older "latest" set is usually a launch-era capture of an object that has since escaped.
  5. Amateur astrometry fits — find_orb fits to NEOCP and pseudo-MPEC observations (the neocpsat catalogue) and dedicated reconstructions (Change3, change2, 25010d). These are the only current tracks for the manoeuvring Chang'e-6 orbiter and Tiandu-1, and for new stages that have no Space-Track elements (Roman's Falcon Heavy stage, MMX's H3 stage).

After DECAY, the source with the freshest epoch wins; ties go in the order above. The object list is cross-checked against the cislunar-eye target catalogue (master list of 2026-10-22).

1.3 Notation

  • COSPAR YYYY-NNNX; NORAD = 5- or 6-digit catalogue number. Bill Gray uses 79xxx / 89xxx surrogates for objects Space-Track has not catalogued.
  • q / Q = perigee / apogee distance from Earth's centre (Bill Gray, Horizons and find_orb fits); alt = altitude above Earth's equatorial radius (Space-Track values). Heliocentric q / Q in AU.
  • Times are UTC unless a fit is quoted in TT. LD = lunar distance, 384 400 km.
  • Source tags: SATCAT, ST-GP (Space-Track GP), BG (Bill Gray TLE), JPL (Horizons, with SPK ID), fit (amateur find_orb fit).
